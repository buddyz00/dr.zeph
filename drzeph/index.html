My Story

A conversation with a friend in fourth grade shaped the mission of my life.

I grew up in a stable home where I felt loved, nurtured, and secure. One day, a friend told me about his parents' divorce—how much it hurt him to see them fight and how difficult it was having to go back and forth between their homes.

I remember thinking:

“I want to help people have less painful relationships.”

I didn't know it then, but that simple desire would eventually lead me through ministry, marriage and family therapy, and global mental health—and shape every aspect of the work I do today.

Professional Bio

Zephon D. Lister, PhD, LMFT

Zephon D. Lister, PhD, LMFT, is a professor, marriage and family therapist, researcher, educator, and speaker whose work focuses on strengthening individuals, relationships, families, and communities.

Dr. Lister is Professor of Counseling and Family Sciences at Loma Linda University. His work spans marriage and family therapy, integrated behavioral health, global and community mental health, chronic illness, resilience, and the intersection of faith, spirituality, and relational well-being.

Before joining the faculty at Loma Linda University, Dr. Lister spent seven years at the University of California, San Diego, where he directed the Collaborative Care Program within Family Medicine and helped expand integrated behavioral health services across the health system.

His research and program development have increasingly focused on making mental-health resources more accessible in communities where traditional models of care may be difficult to sustain. His global mental-health work has included community-based resilience initiatives in Africa, using task-sharing and capacity-building approaches to equip teachers, healthcare workers, community members, and other non-specialists with practical tools for supporting mental and relational well-being. His current research includes resilience initiatives in Sierra Leone and Kenya, as well as published research examining the Community Resiliency Model among survivors and released genocide prisoners in Rwanda.

Dr. Lister's work also examines the intersection of relationships and physical health, particularly chronic illness. He has contributed to research and clinical initiatives involving diabetes and sickle cell disease and is involved in integrated behavioral-health efforts within Loma Linda University Health's Sickle Cell Disease Center.

Across his work as an educator, clinician, researcher, and speaker, Dr. Lister seeks to translate psychological and relational science into practical approaches that help people develop greater capacity, strengthen meaningful relationships, and build healthier, more resilient communities.

Education & Training

Dr. Lister earned his PhD and MS in Marriage and Family Therapy from Loma Linda University, an MA in Religion from Andrews University, and a BA in Theology with a minor in Psychology from Southwestern Adventist University. He completed advanced postdoctoral training in Medical Family Therapy at the Chicago Center for Family Health with John Rolland and Froma Walsh.

He has been a Licensed Marriage and Family Therapist since 2009 and has received recognition including Loma Linda University's Wil Alexander Whole Person Care Award, the Collaborative Family Healthcare Association Wingspread Award, the School of Behavioral Health Research Excellence Award, and the 2025 School of Behavioral Health Faculty Excellence Award for Scholar Researcher.
